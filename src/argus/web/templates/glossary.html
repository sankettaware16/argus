Glossary

The pages

Needs attention
Start here. Follow-ups, investigations that changed since someone reviewed them, and unreviewed ones the rule rates high or critical or ARGUS raised from its own evidence.
Not reviewed
Every investigation nobody has reviewed yet. The AI column shows ARGUS's assessment.
Reviewed
Investigations already decided: the decision, who made it, when.
All
Every investigation. Lists are sorted newest first by default (by the last alert); "most urgent first" and "oldest first" are a click away.
By rule · By host or IP
The same investigations, per detection rule, or per host, IP address, domain or user.
Patterns
Groups of investigations that look alike (same rule, same step results, same key values), to review in one go.
Proposals
Outcomes ARGUS proposes, grouped by rule; open the random audit sample of a group, then confirm the group in one click. Nothing is closed in Kibana.
Tuning
Each rule's weekly report: how much it fires, how often a run hit its alert limit, your verdicts, and suggested changes backed by them. You apply them in Kibana; ARGUS never edits a rule.
Gold set
The answer key: real investigations whose correct outcome you confirmed. ARGUS's scores are measured against it.
Health
Whether data is arriving from the SIEM and any gaps (a gap means unknown, never clean).
Metrics
How the assessments and the AI connection are doing: results, waiting work, refusals, fallbacks, cost and limits.
AI cost (the bar at the top)
What the paid AI provider has cost this month against the monthly cap (config/llm.yaml), and today against the daily cap. Free providers are always asked first; near the monthly cap (80 %) normal work uses only them.

ARGUS's assessment of an investigation

Decision engine
Proposes an outcome only when at least 5 of people's past verdicts on the same kind of alert agree (95%), none older than 30 days. No AI involved.
AI investigator
Reads the evidence with {{ own_words("names") }} and addresses hidden, may ask the SIEM for more (a fixed list of read-only checks), weighs hypotheses with the facts for and against each, and must quote the facts it relies on. Its page shows all of it.
No proposal
ARGUS could not decide, or was not allowed to: it never proposes a harmless outcome while a danger sign (a threat-list hit, success after an attack, a beacon, a rare destination, high severity, something new) is present or unknown. The page says which.
Ask the AI investigator now
On any investigation, reviewed or not: the AI looks at it within a few minutes. The free AI providers are asked first, the paid one only if none can answer (about a US cent).

The full report

E-numbers (E1, E2 …)
Each request ARGUS made to the SIEM for an investigation has its own number, kept for good. Every fact in the report names the requests it comes from; "the alerts" means ARGUS's own store of them.
What ARGUS found with extra SIEM queries
What the SIEM checks added to what the alerts alone say: New facts, checks that found nothing, checks that could not tell (a zero from logs ARGUS cannot yet trust, an out-of-date threat list, a check that waits for another), checks that failed or were not checked, and what is still open, with who can resolve it.
Evidence strength
A count of what ARGUS could check (the signals that block a harmless proposal, the SIEM checks, the rule's steps, the alerts read), separate from any confidence.
Where each fact came from
One line per request: the check, the logs or list it read and the time window, how many rows, whether the answer was complete, the SIEM's server time.

What ARGUS checked

Answered
A step of the rule's own note that the stored alerts (or a check ARGUS made) answer; the values are shown.
Not checked yet
The step needs logs or threat intel that ARGUS does not query yet; it says which check will answer it.
For you
An action or a judgement for a person, such as blocking an address or notifying an owner.
No data
The step names a field that none of the alerts carries.
"Could not be told"
ARGUS does not know, for the reason given: never read it as "no" or "none".
"At least"
A count from incomplete data: the real number may be higher.
